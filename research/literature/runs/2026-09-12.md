## Added publications
- Why Does Post-Training Quantization Work? (2026-09-10; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11716). Explains pretrained LLM quantization robustness through partial cancellation between newly introduced and inherited hidden-state errors and through LM-head geometry that preserves high-ranked token probabilities. Relevant to MetaPAC because it suggests cross-layer error interaction and output-distribution preservation as signals beyond independent layer-wise quantization error.
- A Unified Generalization Framework for Model Merging: Trade-offs, Non-Linearity, and Scaling Laws (2026-01-29; https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21690). Uses stability theory to separate optimization and generalization error in heterogeneous model merging, and analyzes how sparsification can suppress task heterogeneity. Relevant to the MetaPAC model-merging branch as theoretical support for selective delta retention and for constructing merge-friendly compressed experts; latest version checked is v2 from 2026-03-19.
- Model Merging in the Era of Large Language Models: Methods, Applications, and Future Directions (2026-03-10; https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09938). Surveys LLM model merging using the FUSE taxonomy and covers weight averaging, task-vector arithmetic, sparsification-enhanced merging, mixture-of-experts approaches, optimization methods, applications, tools, and benchmarks. Useful as a citation-graph hub and taxonomy source for the MetaPAC SLR and its emerging compression-aware merging branch; latest version checked is v2 from 2026-03-30.

## Main findings
1. PTQ robustness is not purely a local-error phenomenon: the new paper reports partial cancellation between inherited and newly introduced hidden-state errors across layers.
2. Output geometry matters: LM-head structure may preserve high-ranked token scores even when hidden representations differ after quantization.
3. Model-merging sparsification now has an explicit generalization interpretation: suppressing task heterogeneity can tighten theoretical bounds, providing a useful lens for importance-guided delta pruning.
4. The FUSE survey supplies a citation-graph hub spanning averaging, task arithmetic, sparsification-enhanced merging, MoE, optimization, applications, and evaluation.
