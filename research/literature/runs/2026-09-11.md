## Added publications
- LILA: Calibration-Free Structured Pruning of Large Language Models via Latent Spectral Geometry (2026-09-10; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11163). Uses the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance between full and neuron-ablated singular-value distributions as a calibration-free neuron-importance score and extends it to dynamic sparsity-budget allocation. A direct structured-pruning importance and allocation baseline for MetaPAC.
- Accuracy is Not Enough: A Divergence-Based Approach to Evaluate Fidelity Loss in Quantized LLMs (2026-09-07; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07664). Evaluates quantization-induced information loss through divergence between full-vocabulary predictive distributions rather than top-1 accuracy alone. It supplies complementary fidelity metrics for MetaPAC compression evaluation; current version checked is v2 from 2026-09-09.
- On the Interaction Between Model Compression and Test-Time Adaptation (2026-09-03; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03604). Studies how structured compression changes test-time adaptability through representational expressivity and adaptation-subspace compatibility. Relevant to fine-tuning-aware MetaPAC objectives and recovery-preserving compression, although its experiments use ResNet-18 and ViT-Base rather than LLMs.
- Scalable Kronecker-Fisher Approximation: Efficient Hessian Analysis for Billion-Parameter Language Models Compression (2026-09-02; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02451). Provides a scalable cross-layer Fisher/Hessian approximation whose scores correlate with degradation and recovery under quantization and sparsification. A strong deterministic importance-estimation baseline for mixed precision, layer-wise sparsity, and adaptive low-rank allocation.
- FlexPosit: Tunable Fractional Precision for LLM Inference Accelerators (2026-09-04; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04724). Co-designs Posit quantization with a precision-tunable bit-serial accelerator and uses hardware-aligned, sensitivity-guided mixed-precision allocation. A hardware-realizability baseline for translating MetaPAC allocations into measurable throughput and energy gains.
- Auto-FlexSwitch: Efficient Dynamic Model Merging via Learnable Task Vector Compression (2026-04-30; https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28109). Jointly learns task-vector sparsification and bit-width selection by model unit and combines compressed task vectors dynamically through feature-similarity retrieval. This is a close extension baseline connecting MetaPAC-style adaptive mixed compression with model merging.
- Expert Merging: Model Merging with Unsupervised Expert Alignment and Importance-Guided Layer Chunking (2025-09-30; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25712). Learns layer-wise merging coefficients from unlabeled calibration data and allocates finer coefficient chunks to more important layers. Relevant to importance-guided granularity and adaptive action allocation in a multi-expert merging setting.
- When Model Merging Rivals Joint Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning: A Task-Vector Geometry Analysis (2026-07-17; https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16062). Relates task-vector direction, support overlap, and the behavior of TIES/RAM-style merging, finding a setting where sign- and support-based methods collapse toward uniform averaging. Useful for diagnosing when sophisticated importance/conflict allocation is likely to matter.
- CoMerge: Conflict-Driven Preference Optimization for Multi-Task Model Merging (2026-09-02; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02273). Uses conflicts exposed by naive task arithmetic to construct self-supervised preference pairs and optimize lightweight tensor-wise merging coefficients. A strong learned conflict-resolution and action-weight baseline for the model-merging branch.
- CABS+: Efficient and Scalable Model Merging via Conflict-Aware Sparsification and Adaptive Weight Allocation (2026-08-13; https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12842). Combines conflict-aware structured sparsification with gradient-free adaptive merging-weight allocation and proposes a mergeability score for model selection. Directly relevant to coupling selective parameter retention with downstream allocation.

## Main directions
1. Calibration-free spectral importance: LILA scores structured-pruning importance using distributional changes in singular spectra and converts those scores into sparsity budgets.
2. Compression-aware merging: Auto-FlexSwitch and CABS+ couple sparsification with learned or searched allocation rather than treating task-vector compression as a fixed pre-processing step.
3. Cross-layer sensitivity: Kronecker-Fisher approximations provide a scalable deterministic alternative to a learned MetaPAC importance predictor.
4. Hardware-realizable precision: FlexPosit explicitly co-designs mixed-precision decisions with a bit-serial accelerator.
5. Evaluation beyond task accuracy: predictive-distribution divergence and adaptability under distribution shift may expose degradation hidden by ordinary accuracy.
6. Learned conflict resolution: CoMerge and Expert Merging learn fine-grained merging coefficients from conflict or hidden-state/logit alignment signals.
