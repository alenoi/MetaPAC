## New publications

1. **Michael Wang, Keith Li, Roozbeh Bostandoost — MetaKV: Adaptive KV Cache Compression for Constrained LLM Inference**  
   arXiv:2609.07966, submitted 2026-09-07. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07966>  
   MetaKV predicts correctness, latency, and peak memory for candidate KV-cache configurations and selects a prompt-specific option satisfying user constraints. Its predictor-to-budgeted-allocator structure makes it the closest new adjacent MetaPAC baseline, while its actions operate on runtime KV state rather than model weights.

2. **Lexington Whalen, Yuki Ito, Ryo Sakamoto — KBBQ: A Predictive Noise Law and the Limits of Spectrum Flattening in FP4 Quantization**  
   arXiv:2609.08135, submitted 2026-09-08. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08135>  
   KBBQ derives an analytic second-order FP4 quantization-noise model using a participation factor and closed-form SNR law. It is a useful deterministic error proxy against which MetaPAC's learned importance estimates can be compared.

3. **Jed A. Duersch, Naïm Es-Sebbani, Nathanaël Haas, Zied Bouraoui — Dense Structural Compression of Transformers via Gauge-Correct Channel Removal**  
   arXiv:2609.07264, submitted 2026-09-07. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07264>  
   The paper learns physically removable transformer channels with gauge-correct group penalties and per-channel inference-utility calibration. It is relevant to structured importance estimation and dense deployable compaction, though the reported scope is not centered on large LLMs.

4. **Zhixiong Zhao, Zukang Xu, Guangyu Sun, Lifeng Liu, Dawei Yang — All for 1-Bit: Towards Genuine 1-Bit Post-Training Quantization for LLMs**  
   EMNLP 2026 Main; arXiv:2609.06161, submitted 2026-09-05. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06161>  
   The method targets strict 1.0-bit-per-weight PTQ using Hessian-aware reconstruction and hierarchical Shapley sensitivity for capacity allocation. This is a strong direct importance-and-allocation baseline under extreme quantization.

5. **Sieun Hyeon, Jaeyoung Do — Beyond Retraining-Free MoE Compression: A Cost-Normalized Study of Post-Compression Adjustment**  
   EMNLP 2026 Main; arXiv:2609.06076, submitted 2026-09-05. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06076>  
   This study compares language-model fine-tuning with teacher knowledge distillation after MoE pruning or merging under matched limited-data and compute budgets. It provides a controlled baseline for MetaPAC's recovery stage across MoE compression choices.

6. **Hannah Laus, Claudio Mayrink Verdun, Hao Wang, Flavio du Pin Calmon, Felix Krahmer — KV Cache Compression Through the Lens of Transform Coding**  
   arXiv:2608.14191, submitted 2026-08-14. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14191>  
   Found through the KV-cache allocation graph, it derives attention-aware distortion and uses reverse water-filling to allocate bits across tokens and channels. It is a strong deterministic allocator baseline for extending MetaPAC's decision framework to runtime state.

## Main directions and baseline assessment

The strongest new direction is prompt-adaptive, multi-objective compression selection: **MetaKV** operationalizes a predictor-to-allocator pipeline under simultaneous quality, latency, and memory constraints. **All for 1-Bit** is the strongest new weight-compression baseline because it couples an explicit sensitivity model with structural capacity allocation. **KBBQ** and the gauge-correct channel-removal work broaden the deterministic importance/proxy baseline family, while the MoE adjustment study strengthens evaluation of recovery objectives. The transform-coding paper connects global budget allocation and reverse water-filling to KV-cache compression.
