## Added publications

1. **ESTS at WMT26: Routing-Informed Expert Pruning for Model Compression** — Liu O. Martin, Lucas Bandarkar, Nanyun Peng; WMT 2026; arXiv:2609.12310; submitted 2026-09-11. Uses task-specific routing mass for expert importance, cross-lingual routing divergence for layer-wise retained-capacity allocation, physical expert removal, recovery tuning, and MXFP4 quantization.
2. **RPTQ: Reorder-based Post-training Quantization for Large Language Models** — Zhihang Yuan et al.; arXiv:2304.01089; submitted 2023-04-03. Reorders and clusters activation channels by range and fuses the permutation into existing operations.
3. **ShortGPT: Layers in Large Language Models are More Redundant Than You Expect** — Xin Men et al.; arXiv:2403.03853; submitted 2024-03-06. Uses Block Influence to rank and remove entire transformer layers.
4. **Atom: Low-bit Quantization for Efficient and Accurate LLM Serving** — Yilong Zhao et al.; arXiv:2310.19102; submitted 2023-10-29. Couples fine-grained mixed-precision W4A4 quantization with low-bit GPU operators for serving.

All four relevance assessments are based on abstracts and primary indexed metadata; full-text assessment is pending.

## New directions and baseline implications

The main new direction is **hierarchical mixed compression for MoE models**: importance is estimated at expert level, the retained budget is allocated across layers, low-importance experts are physically removed, and the survivors are quantized. ESTS is therefore a direct related method/baseline for extending MetaPAC beyond dense transformers. RPTQ is a clustering-based activation-quantization baseline, ShortGPT a deterministic block-importance pruning baseline, and Atom a hardware-aware execution baseline.
